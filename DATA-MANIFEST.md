# Data manifest: nonprofit density database, 2000–2024

Draft of 2026-11-17. This file documents every source in the county × year and
CBSA × year panels (`data/analysis/{county,cbsa}_year.parquet`): how each was
acquired, its version, the years covered, and the slice kept. It then defines
the sampling frame and lists the breaks in continuity a user must know about.

Year-by-year tables are generated by `R/build/F_checks/10_source_coverage.R` and written to
`data/diagnostics/manifest_*.csv`. Rerun that script after any rebuild of 06.

Contents

1. Geography and population
2. Nonprofit sources (BMF, CORE, SOI, e-file)
3. Covariate sources
4. Sampling frame: registered, filers, grassroots, expenditures
5. Filers by return type and paper vs e-file, by year
6. Breaks in continuity
7. Software and version pins

### 2.4 Rejected source versions

**NCCS CORE "marts" `-V1` files (PZ and CE), e.g.
`harmonized/core/501c3-pz/marts/CORE-YYYY-501C3-CHARITIES-PZ-HRMN-V1.csv` and
`501ce-pz/marts/CORE-YYYY-501CE-NONPROFIT-PZ-HRMN-V1.csv`, last modified
2025-04-11.**
- Every money field is stored as a tiny denormalized double, around 1.1e-308. Revenue, expenses, assets, contributions and the rest are all affected.
- Only exact zeros survive. Example from TY2015, first row: total assets `1.11253692925969e-308`, total expenses `1.11253692925669e-308`.
- The pattern looks like 64-bit integers written out as raw double bit patterns, which also loses precision in the CSV text.
- `03_npo_core.R` uses the unversioned `harmonized/core/501c3-pz/CORE-YYYY-…-PZ-HRMN.csv` files instead (last modified 2025-03-04). It stops if max |revenue| < 1.
- The PF marts (`-V0`) are numerically fine, but nearly empty for TY2016–18.
- This should be reported to NCCS.

### 2.5 NTEE codes: invalid-code backfill (2026-11-17)

**Rule** (one copy since 2026-11-17: `bad_ntee()` / `resolve_ntee()` in `R/lib/00_setup.R`. `02_npo_bmf_counts.R` writes the lookup `data/npo/ein_ntee_best.csv.gz` and applies it to the legacy snapshots; 02b, 06, 08, 23 and 24 apply it to their records. Codes it cannot resolve are set to NA):
- A record's 3-character NTEE is **invalid** if it is missing, blank, or starts with `UND` (undefined), `INV` (invalid) or `Z99` (unknown).
- An invalid code is replaced by the **most recent valid NTEE for the same EIN in the legacy NCCS BMF snapshots** (2000–2022-08, `ein_location_bmf.csv.gz`). There, NTEE = `NTEEFINAL`, else `NTEECC`.
- The backfilled code then drives the hospital/university flag (E20–E24, B40–B43, B50) and the human-services flag (I–P).

**Before the backfill**, NTEE was taken in this order:
1. The BMF snapshot of the same EIN-year (CORE and SOI-PF records).
2. The record's own code: SOI `NTEE_IRS`, else the NTEEV2 middle segment. For e-file, `ntee_code_clean` from the unified-BMF join in `panel990`.

**Why it was needed:**
- About 16% of active c3 e-file filers in 2020–24 carry "UND".
- Before the fix, about 590 hospitals and universities a year stayed in the H&U-excluded series, which was about 6.5% of `exp_c3_nohu` and moved Winchester VA by 1.6 log points.
- `*_hs` counts were 12–13% low.
- National ratios new/old for 2022: `exp_c3_nohu` 0.934, `n_filer_c3_nohu` 0.998, `n_filer_c3_hs` 1.14.

**Limits:**
- Organizations first registered after August 2022 have no legacy snapshot and keep "UND".
- **Registered counts (BMF), fixed 2026-11-17.** `02b_npo_bmf_monthly.R` now applies the same rule. In the December monthly files, 25% of located public charities are `UNDEFINED`/`INVALID`: 349,272 in 2023 and 343,629 in 2024. Of these, 308,967 (88.5%) and 301,361 (87.7%) are backfilled (`bmf_monthly_ntee_backfill.csv`). The rule is also applied to the legacy EIN-years in the reconstructed stock: 45,081 of 364,954 invalid EIN-years are recovered, the rest have no valid code in any snapshot.
  - **Effect** (`bmf_ntee_backfill_effect.csv`): `n_bmf_c3pc_hs` for 2023 goes from 346,814 to 389,535. As a share of the registered stock, that is 24.8% → 27.8%, against 27.0% in 2022. 2000–2022 values move by less than 0.5%, and `n_bmf_c3pc_nohu` by less than 0.2%.
  - **Changed in the analysis panels:** only `n_bmf_c3pc_nohu`, `n_bmf_c3pc_hs` and their `_snap` versions. No analysis script or multiverse outcome uses them.
  - **Residual:** the 2022→2023 share still rises 0.7 points, against ±0.3 in earlier years. This reflects the monthly file's `ntee_code_clean` replacing the legacy `NTEEFINAL`, plus about 40k EINs with no valid code anywhere, which count as non-human-services.
  - **Backup** of the earlier outputs: `data/_pre_bmfntee_20261117/`.
- **Form 1023-EZ approvals were checked as a second source and not used** (`R/build/F_checks/10b_ntee_1023ez_check.R`, `ntee_missing_vs_1023ez.csv`).
  - Source: the IRS approval files for 2014–2023 (526,723 approvals; 98% carry a valid applicant-reported NTEE). They come from the NCCS mirror `raw/1023-ez-approvals/f1023ez-approvals-baseline-2014-2023.zip`. Its 2022 and 2023 files are byte-identical to the current IRS files. The IRS link for 2024 returned 404 on 2026-11-17.
  - **Coverage:** few of the missing codes are in the files. Of the Dec 2024 public charities with a missing code (343,629), 8,212 have a 1023-EZ code (2.4%), and only 6,886 of those are not already recovered from the BMF. Among active e-file filers, 1023-EZ adds 257–780 codes a year. Most of the residual is out of reach: 27,441 of the 42,268 Dec 2024 public charities still missing a code after the BMF backfill were ruled before the 1023-EZ existed (2014).
  - **Quality:** where both sources have a code, the self-reported 1023-EZ code matches the BMF code on 30–44% of 3-character codes and on 65–68% of major groups.
  - **Effect if used:** it would add about 2,700 human-services organizations in 2024, +0.7% to `n_bmf_c3pc_hs`.
- **The NCCS NTEE-resolved crosswalk was checked and not used** (`R/build/F_checks/10c_ntee_resolved_check.R`, `ntee_missing_vs_resolved.csv`).
  - Source: `crosswalks/ntee-resolved/ntee_resolved_crosswalk.parquet`, last modified 2026-09-21. It has one row per EIN (3.62M), with the most recent and modal NTEE across the legacy snapshots plus monthly BMF vintages to July 2026.
  - **It reproduces the build's backfill.** Where both have a code, it matches the legacy-snapshot code on 99.7–100% of 3-character codes.
  - **It adds almost nothing.** Among active e-file filers it adds 127–145 codes a year (3–6% of the residual). Among Dec 2023 and Dec 2024 public charities it adds 570 and 338 (1.4% and 0.8% of the residual), mostly from 2026 monthly vintages. In Dec 2024 that would be 147 more human-services organizations.
  - **The residual has never been coded.** Of the 41,930 Dec 2024 public charities still missing a code after both, only 4,186 appear in the crosswalk at all, and those 4,186 carry only invalid codes.
- Health systems coded outside E20–E24 (E31, E03) are not caught; this is a coding issue, not a missing code.

## 4. Sampling frame

The unit is an organization (EIN) in a year. Every count and dollar total is the
sum, over the county-equivalents of a CBSA, of organizations located there. Five
nested frames are used.

| Frame | Variable(s) | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Registered** | `n_bmf_c3pc` | 501(c)(3) public charities in the IRS BMF stock: subsection 03, foundation code not 02/03/04, reconstructed stock (2.1). The stock includes organizations that file nothing or a 990-N, churches that chose to register, and group-exemption subordinates. |
| **Filers** | `n_filer_c3` | 501(c)(3) organizations whose 990 or 990-EZ for the tax year is in the spliced financial source, located to a county, and **active**: max(total revenue, total expenses) ≥ **$50,000 in 2010 dollars** (PCE). The floor is $40,780 nominal in 2000, $50,000 in 2010, $57,818 in 2020 and $68,311 in 2024. 990-PF filers are excluded. `n_filer_c3_nofloor` counts every c3 990/EZ record, with no floor. |
| **Grassroots** | `n_grass = n_bmf_c3pc − n_filer_c3` | Registered public charities that are not active filers. This group includes 990-N e-postcard filers (gross receipts normally ≤ $50k from TY2010, ≤ $25k for TY2007–09), 990/EZ filers below the floor, churches and others not required to file, subordinates covered by a group return, and organizations heading for automatic revocation. It is built at metro level in `94_variables_panel_model.R`, and can be ≤ 0 in a few small metros. |
| **Expenditures** | `exp_c3` (also `rev_c3`, `con_c3`, `assets_c3`) | Total expenses of active c3 filers, in 2022 dollars, with negatives set to 0. The line is Part I line 18 / Part IX line 25 on the 990, and line 17 on the 990-EZ. `exp_c3_net` subtracts grants to domestic organizations and governments (Part IX line 1; EZ line 10 as an upper bound) from 2012 on. |
| **Excluding hospitals and universities (H&U)** | `n_filer_c3_nohu`, `exp_c3_nohu`, `rev_c3_nohu`, `n_bmf_c3pc_nohu` | The same frames, dropping NTEE **E20–E24** (hospitals) and **B40–B43, B50** (higher education). SOI and e-file records are also dropped when the NTEEV2 industry is HOS or UNI. |

Related frames:
- **Human services** `*_hs`: NTEE major groups I–P.
- **All 501(c)** `n_filer_all`: every subsection, active.
- **Foundations** `n_pf`, `pf_assets`, `pf_grants`: every 990-PF filer, with no floor. Assets are book value at end of year, in 2022 $.

NTEE codes come from the BMF snapshot for the same EIN-year when one exists,
otherwise from the record. Invalid codes are backfilled from the BMF (2.5, 6.16).
Integrated health systems coded outside E20–E24 stay in the H&U-excluded series.
For example, Kaiser is E31, booked in Oakland at $71B, and Cleveland Clinic is
E03. So `exp_c3_nohu` in a few metros reflects where a headquarters sits.

Which frame answers which question:
- **Filers** measure the formal, fiscally active sector.
- **Grassroots** measure small and informal registered organizations. It is the cleanest count outcome for niche measures, because filer counts are tied to the concentration indices.
- **Expenditures** measure resources. H&U account for 51–56% of active c3 expenses in every year, which is why the H&U-excluded spending series is the default (shares by year in Table 5.1).

## 5. Filers by return type and paper vs e-file, by year

### 5.1 The frames by year (national, all county-equivalents)

Counts are organizations. Dollars are billions of 2022 $. Grassroots =

## 6. Breaks in continuity

The breaks are listed in chronological order. "Policy" items are rules that
changed who files what. "Data" items are artifacts of the sources or the splice.
Each item names the series it affects and how the build handles it.

**6.1 2000–2007: pre-redesign Form 990 (policy).**
- Before TY2008, a 501(c)(3) could file the 990-EZ if gross receipts were < $100k and assets < $250k. Organizations with receipts normally ≤ $25k filed nothing.
- CORE files for TY2000–07 carry no return-type field, so 990 and EZ cannot be told apart (Table 5.2).
- The pre-2008 Part II has no split of grants to organizations vs individuals, so net-of-pass-through measures cannot start before 2008.

**6.2 2000: BMF timing (data).** The 2000 registered count comes from the
May 2000 snapshot, 7.6 months before year end. 2020 uses the April 2020
snapshot, 8.6 months early (`bmf_months_off`).

**6.3 2006: Pension Protection Act (policy).**
- It created the 990-N e-postcard for tax years beginning after 2006 (first due in 2008).
- It created automatic revocation after three consecutive years of non-filing (IRC 6033(j)), with the first revocations effective May 15, 2010.
- From 2008, the smallest organizations become visible to the IRS (990-N) but never enter the financial files. They belong to the grassroots frame.

**6.4 TY2008–2010: Form 990 redesign and EZ threshold phase-in (policy).**
- The redesigned core 990 plus Schedules A–R apply from TY2008. Revenue (Part VIII) and expense (Part IX) lines were renumbered. The harmonized `F9_` names in CORE, SOI and e-file map both versions.
- EZ eligibility tightened in three steps:

  | Tax year | Gross receipts below | Assets below |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2008 | $1M | $2.5M |
  | 2009 | $500k | $1.25M |
  | 2010+ | $200k | $500k |

  Our data show the change: the 95th percentile of gross receipts among CORE EZ returns falls from $643k (2008) to $367k (2009) to $175k (2010).
- **Effect:** mid-size organizations move from EZ to the full 990. Active-filer counts are unaffected, because the floor uses revenue and expenses, not form type. Fields that exist only on the full 990 (wages, Part IX grants) gain coverage.

**6.5 TY2010: 990-N threshold raised from $25k to $50k (policy).**
Organizations with receipts of $25k–$50k leave the EZ files for the e-postcard.
This moves `n_filer_c3_nofloor` down and grassroots up. Active filers move little,
because the $50k (2010 $) floor sits at the same point.

**6.6 2010–2011: automatic revocations (policy, large data effect).**
- The IRS published the first revocation list on June 8, 2011, with about 275,000 organizations that had not filed for 2007–09.
- The reconstructed registered stock falls from 1,061,178 (2010) to 998,747 (2011), −5.9%. Implied exits (stock[t−1] + entries[t] − stock[t]) spike in 2011.
- Reinstated organizations get a new ruling date. The entry series therefore uses each EIN's *first* ruling date (long BMF panel), and drops monthly-BMF rulings for EINs already seen in an earlier snapshot.
- **Use:** the registered and grassroots frames carry a level shift at 2011. Treat 2010→2011 changes as administrative, not real exit; use year fixed effects.

**6.7 2011: splice from CORE to SOI (data).**
- In the overlap years 2011–2019, SOI has **3–11% more active c3 filers** than CORE (national ratio 1.03–1.11) and 1–5% more expenses (`splice_overlap.csv`).
- Counts therefore jump at 2011 (flag `post_splice_soi`). Metro levels are highly correlated across sources (log r > .99), so cross-sections and year fixed effects absorb most of it. Raw 2000–2024 trends in filer counts do not.

**6.8 2011–2015: grants to organizations incomplete (data).**
- In CORE 2010 and SOI 2011, only about 7% of filers report Part IX line 1, against 18–20% from 2012.
- The SOI 990-EZ extract has no grants line.
- Net-of-pass-through series (`*_net`) therefore start in **2012**, and SOI-era EZ grants count as zero.

**6.9 July 1, 2014: Form 1023-EZ (policy).**
- Organizations projecting gross receipts ≤ $50k and assets ≤ $250k can apply with a short online form and little review.
- New 501(c)(3) rulings jump from 43,960 (2013) to 77,251 (2014) in the long BMF panel, and stay near 60–78k a year afterwards.
- **Effect:** the registered stock and grassroots frames grow faster from 2014 on, mostly through very small organizations. Filer counts are barely affected, because most of these organizations sit below the floor. Entry series carry this break; consider a post-2014 indicator.

**6.10 2016–2018: legacy BMF snapshots omit new organizations (data).**
- The 2016–2018 NCCS legacy snapshots leave out most newly ruled organizations. The 2019 file adds about 214k EINs ruled 2016–19.
- The raw snapshot stock dips and then jumps in 2019. The reconstructed stock (`n_bmf_*`) back-fills them; the raw counts are kept as `n_bmf_*_snap` for comparison.
- The back-fill only covers organizations ruled within 3 years of first appearing, or ruled 2016+. A residual jump remains: the reconstructed stock still rises 7.6% in 2019 (Table 5.1), from older or group-subordinate EINs that first appear in the 2019 file.
- **New foundations ruled 2016–21 appear only from the 2023 monthly BMF.** So `n_bmf_pf` steps from 96,048 (2022) to 137,922 (2023), and `n_entry_pf` is NA for 2016–21. The foundation stock is not comparable across 2022/2023.

**6.11 TY2016–2018: no complete 990-PF source (data).**
- The IRS published no 990-PF extract for filing years 2017–2019.
- CORE PF is nearly empty for TY2016–18.
- Foundation counts, assets and grants are interpolated linearly by county between 2015 and 2019 (`pf_interp = TRUE`, `src_pf = "INTERP"`). Do not use these years for foundation dynamics.

**6.12 TY2018: Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (policy; covariate break).**
- TCJA roughly doubled the standard deduction for TY2018–2025.
- In the IRS SOI county data, the itemizer share falls from 31% to 11.5% nationally (about 20% to 5% in the median county). Median contribution per itemizing return falls from $1,227 to $804.
- Metro ranks survive (rank r = .966, 2017→18), but contribution covariates have different meaning before and after 2018. The partial r of contributions rises .03–.06 after 2018 (`84_tcja_contrib.R`).
- Some giving was likely pulled forward into TY2017.
- This is a covariate break, not a nonprofit-count break. All multiverse cross-sections (2019, 2022) are post-TCJA.

**6.13 2019: BMF coding change (data).** The 2019+ legacy files code
`SUBSECCD` and `FNDNCD` as "3.0" / "4.0". These codes are normalized; read
literally they would turn foundations into public charities.

**6.14 July 2019 onward: Taxpayer First Act e-file mandate (policy).**
- Before the mandate, e-filing was required only of organizations with ≥ $10M in assets filing ≥ 250 returns a year ($100M before TY2006), and of private foundations filing ≥ 250 returns.
- The Taxpayer First Act (July 1, 2019) requires e-filing of the 990 and 990-PF for tax years beginning after July 1, 2019. The 990-EZ followed for tax years ending on or after July 31, 2021, after transition relief.
- Form 1023 must be e-filed from January 31, 2020.
- **Effect:** the e-file universe is near-complete from TY2020 for the 990 and from TY2021 for the EZ. The paper share by year is in Table 5.3.

**6.15 TY2020: splice from SOI to e-file, and COVID-19 (data and policy).**
- 990/EZ switch to e-file in 2020, because SOI's 2020 EZ extract is short (about 154k rows vs about 220k).
- E-file has 3% fewer active c3 filers than SOI in 2020 (ratio 0.970). It is 1–3% above SOI in 2021–22.
- TY2020 active filers are 274,865, against 300,574 in 2019 (−8.6%). Almost all of the gap is in the 990-EZ: 55.7k active EZ filers vs 79.7k in 2019 (Table 5.2), while full-990 filers are flat. Two causes: EZ filers not yet under the mandate filed on paper, and COVID deadline relief (returns due May 15, 2020 moved to July 15, 2020) plus pandemic disruption.
- Revenue in 2020–21 also includes PPP loans forgiven as contributions or grants, and pandemic relief grants.
- **Use:** flag 2020 (`post_splice_efile`). For trend endpoints, prefer 2019 and 2022.

**6.16 2020–2024: e-file NTEE "UND" (data, fixed 2026-11-17).**
- About 16% of active c3 e-file filers carry NTEE "UND" (undefined) from the NCCS unified BMF join.
- Before the fix, about 590 hospitals and universities a year stayed in the H&U-excluded series, and `*_hs` counts were 12–13% low.
- Fix: replace with the most recent valid BMF code for the EIN (2.5). Of every e-file record with an invalid code (all 501(c) subsections, any size:
181k–195k a year, about 29% of records), 90–94% are recovered
(`ntee_backfill.csv`). The rest are mostly EINs that first appear after the last
legacy snapshot (2022-08). They stay coded "UND", so they count as neither H&U
nor human services.
- Multiverse runs before v11 (v1–v10) predate the fix.

**6.17 2022/2023: BMF source switch, and entries from survivors (data).**
- From 2023, registered counts come from the NCCS monthly BMF: no `OUTNCCS` screen, 50 states + DC (`bmf_src_monthly = 1`). The reconstructed public-charity stock moves from 1,417,504 (2022) to 1,401,402 (2023).
- Entries for 2022–24 count only organizations still registered in December 2024. In the overlap years 2015–21 that is 63–66% of all entrants, so entry levels from 2022 are not comparable with 2021 (`entry_src_monthly`).

**6.18 Geography (data).**
- Connecticut moved from 8 counties to 9 planning regions (Census adoption 2022). All years are rebuilt on planning regions from town estimates; Connecticut metros are dropped from the 1970–1990 history.
- Other boundary changes are listed in Section 1.
- CBSA membership is fixed at the 2023 delineation for every year.
- BLS nonprofit MSA data were published on the 2013 and 2018 delineations (`bls_delin_flag`).

**6.19 Covariate coverage breaks (data).**
- **BEA:** SIC to NAICS in 2001 (2000 read from CAINC5S). Employment and transfers end in 2022.
- **ACS 5-year:** starts with end year 2009. 2001–08 are filled in `30_assemble` (Census 2000 SF3 for 2000).
- **Charitable deductions:** the IRS SOI county data start in TY2010.
- **Social capital:** Rupasingha ends in 2014 and is carried forward. Category counts are NA after 2014.
- **RCMS:** the number of reporting bodies grows 149 → 236 → 372 across 2000/2010/2020, and historically Black denominations are largely absent in 2000 (`rel_2000_noblack`).
- **Census of Governments 2022:** the public-use file drops salaries and folds some categories together (definitions restricted to stable sums).
- **QCEW 2000:** the file omits suppressed county rows instead of flagging them.
- **LAUS:** ends in 2023.
- **Elections:** presidential years only.
- **Patents:** application years 2021+ are truncated by grant lag.
